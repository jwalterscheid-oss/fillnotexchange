Fill vs exchange

Fill

They put propane into your cylinder. You keep the tank. You pay for the gallons they add. Leftover propane in your tank stays yours.

Look for a bulk tank and a certified attendant. U-Haul, Tractor Supply, Ace Hardware fill desks, and independent dealers are the usual fill points on this site.

30-, 40-, and 100-lb bottles, forklift cylinders, and RV onboard tanks can only be filled — exchange cages will not take them.

Exchange

A locked cage (often Blue Rhino or AmeriGas). You leave a 20-lb grill tank and take a different one. You do not get credit for propane left in your old tank.

Major exchange brands commonly put about 15 lb of propane in a 20-lb cylinder (disclosed on many cages). A refill station fills your tank toward the legal maximum instead of handing you a pre-filled swap.

Cages do not fill 30/40/100-lb bottles, forklifts, or the tank bolted to an RV.

U-Haul’s own refill-vs-exchange tip page is a clear first-party writeup of the same tradeoff (more propane kept, leftover fuel not thrown away). We do not republish their prices. U-Haul: Propane Refill vs. Exchange.

How to read the collar stamp

Look at the metal collar or handle around the valve. Dates are stamped into the metal, not a paper sticker. This is how to read a stamp — not how to fill a tank. We do not publish DIY fill instructions.

  • Original manufacture (12-year stamp): usually month then year, like 08 14 for August 2014. For a typical portable DOT grill cylinder, that first stamp is good for 12 years. After that a licensed filler should refuse until a DOT requalifier puts a new date on the collar. Source: PHMSA “Is Your Cylinder Safe to Fill?” consumer sheet and 49 CFR 180.209.
  • An E recert: after requalification you often see a newer month-year plus the letter E, sometimes with a RIN (requalifier ID). E means an external visual inspection by an approved requalifier. That next interval is 5 years.
  • An S stamp: proof-pressure test. PHMSA’s consumer sheet marks the next interval as 10 years.
  • No letter after the requalification date: volumetric expansion (water-jacket) test — next interval 12 years on the PHMSA sheet.
  • A fill-station “visual check” before they pump is not a DOT recert. You need a requalifier stamp or laser etch on the collar.

PHMSA PDF: Is Your Cylinder Safe to Fill? (PHH50-0201). Read that sheet for the official diagrams. Rules can change — the stamp and the filler on duty are what count today.

Why a refill station may turn you away

  • Collar date past due and no current requalification stamp.
  • Excessive rust, dents, bulges, a damaged collar/footring, or a bad valve — PHMSA’s sheet says do not fill those.
  • Wrong tank for that pump (for example a station that only does 20-lb portable cylinders).
  • No certified attendant on duty (common late in the day — call first).
  • You brought an exchange-cage expectation to a fill pump, or a fill expectation to a locked cage.
